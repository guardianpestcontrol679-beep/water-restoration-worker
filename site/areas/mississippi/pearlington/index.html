What causes water, fire and mold damage in Pearlington

Pearlington shares the weather and building patterns of the rest of the state. Mississippi is exposed to Gulf hurricanes, frequent tornadoes, flash flooding and Mississippi River crests, and the humid climate means wet crawlspaces and attics develop mold rapidly. Many homes sit on piers over crawlspaces that collect moisture year-round. Problems tend to peak during spring severe weather and hurricane season, but plumbing failures and appliance leaks happen in every month of the year.

Typical emergencies in the area:

  • Intense rainfall that overwhelms gutters, downspouts and yard drainage
  • Water heater tank failures that release 40 to 80 gallons at once
  • Persistent humidity that slows natural drying and lets damp materials turn into mold problems
  • Flash flooding that pushes water through doors, window wells and foundation walls in minutes
  • Standing water and wet insulation in crawlspaces
  • Toilet and tub overflows
  • Lightning strikes and downed power lines during severe storms
  • Lightning-caused attic and roof fires
  • Air-conditioning condensate leaks and sweating ductwork in closets and attics
  • Flood water that was never fully dried out of walls and floors

First steps for Pearlington property owners

The first hour determines how much can be saved. While a crew is on the way:

  • Do not run the HVAC system if ducts or the air handler got wet, and do not use household fans on sewage or flood water
  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a plumbing failure, and turn off power to the affected area if water is near outlets or appliances
  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything
  • After a fire, wait for the fire department to release the property and do not wipe soot with water, which smears it into surfaces
  • If you see or smell mold, avoid disturbing it and keep the area closed off until it is assessed
  • Move rugs, furniture legs and valuables out of the water and off wet floors

Pearlington restoration FAQs

What should I do about a sewage backup in Pearlington?

Keep people and pets out of the area, do not run water in the house until the blockage is cleared, and call for professional cleanup. Sewage is Category 3 water and requires protective equipment, removal of porous materials and hospital-grade disinfection.
