Restoration services available throughout Michigan

  • Water damage restoration: extraction, structural drying, mold prevention and rebuild after burst pipes, appliance failures, roof leaks and flooding.
  • Fire damage restoration: emergency board-up, suppression water removal, soot and smoke cleaning, odor elimination and reconstruction.
  • Mold remediation: inspection, containment, HEPA filtration, safe removal and moisture correction under the IICRC S520 standard.
  • Sewage, storm and biohazard cleanup: contaminated-water protocols, disinfection and lawful disposal.

What to expect from a Michigan restoration crew

Every crew we connect you with in Michigan is a locally operated company. Expect a call back within minutes, an arrival window measured in hours for active emergencies, a moisture or damage assessment on arrival, a written scope and photo documentation that supports your claim. You are free to ask about licensing and certifications, and you should.

Insurance notes for Michigan property owners

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and fire, exclude flooding from rising water (which requires a separate flood policy) and gradual leaks, and often treat sewer backup and mold as optional or limited coverages. Frozen pipe claims may be denied if a home was left unheated, so check your policy's freeze provisions before winter travel.

Michigan restoration FAQs

When is damage most common in Michigan?

Winter and the spring thaw is the busiest period for restoration crews in Michigan. Michigan homes almost always have basements, and sewer backups and sump failures during intense summer rain, plus burst pipes and ice dams in winter, make up most of the state's water damage. Lake-effect snow and heavy roof loads add to the risk on the west side of the state.

Are the restoration companies in Michigan licensed?

They are independent businesses responsible for their own licensing and insurance. Ask for proof of liability insurance, any license Michigan requires for the work (some states license mold remediators specifically) and IICRC certification.

Does Water Restoration handle commercial properties in Michigan?

Yes. Offices, retail, restaurants, warehouses, multifamily and other commercial buildings across Michigan are served by companies with commercial-scale crews and equipment.
