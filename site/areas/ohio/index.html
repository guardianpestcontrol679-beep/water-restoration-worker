Restoration services available throughout Ohio

  • Water damage restoration: extraction, structural drying, mold prevention and rebuild after burst pipes, appliance failures, roof leaks and flooding.
  • Fire damage restoration: emergency board-up, suppression water removal, soot and smoke cleaning, odor elimination and reconstruction.
  • Mold remediation: inspection, containment, HEPA filtration, safe removal and moisture correction under the IICRC S520 standard.
  • Sewage, storm and biohazard cleanup: contaminated-water protocols, disinfection and lawful disposal.

What to expect from a Ohio restoration crew

Restoration companies in Ohio are independent local businesses. When you call (888) 755-3821, we route you to one that covers your ZIP code and has crews available. They assess the damage, stop further loss, give you a written scope and document everything for your insurance claim. Confirm licensing, insurance and IICRC certification before work begins, as you would with any contractor.

Insurance notes for Ohio property owners

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and fire, exclude flooding from rising water (which requires a separate flood policy) and gradual leaks, and often treat sewer backup and mold as optional or limited coverages. Frozen pipe claims may be denied if a home was left unheated, so check your policy's freeze provisions before winter travel.

Ohio restoration FAQs

How quickly do crews respond in Ohio?

Active water and fire emergencies are typically scheduled for same-day response, often within hours. Response in rural parts of Ohio may take longer because crews cover a wider radius, so call as early as possible.

Are the restoration companies in Ohio licensed?

They are independent businesses responsible for their own licensing and insurance. Ask for proof of liability insurance, any license Ohio requires for the work (some states license mold remediators specifically) and IICRC certification.

When is damage most common in Ohio?

Winter and the spring rainy season is the busiest period for restoration crews in Ohio. Ohio's older housing stock, nearly universal basements and heavy spring rain make sewer backups, sump failures and foundation seepage the most common water losses, with burst pipes and ice dams every winter. Lake-effect snow in the northeast and river flooding along the Ohio add seasonal risk.
