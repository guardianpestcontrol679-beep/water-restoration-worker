Soot, odor and water: three clocks running at once

Three forms of damage continue after the flames are out. Soot residue corrodes and stains surfaces progressively. Odor molecules migrate deeper into porous materials. And suppression water, often hundreds of gallons, swells, delaminates and eventually grows mold in everything it touched. Early stabilization changes what can be saved.

That is why restoration companies in Pensacola aim to be on site within a day or two of the fire even when reconstruction will take months. Board-up protects the property; extraction and drying stop the water damage; and the first cleaning pass removes the most corrosive residue.

From board-up to rebuild

  1. Dispatch and assessment. A local crew confirms the property is safe to enter and plans the stabilization.
  2. Securing the structure. Broken openings are boarded, damaged roofing tarped and the site secured.
  3. Water removal and drying. Suppression water is extracted and the structure dried to prevent secondary damage and mold.
  4. Structural cleaning. Walls, ceilings, cabinets, fixtures and the HVAC system are cleaned using methods chosen for wet, dry or protein smoke.
  5. Contents processing. Furniture, clothing, electronics and documents are inventoried, cleaned where possible and stored until the home is ready.
  6. Deodorization at the source, sealing of framing and subfloor, and reconstruction of damaged areas to code.

Contents: what is cleanable and what is not

Hard, non-porous items clean up well; porous items that absorbed heavy smoke usually do not. A reputable crew will test-clean before committing to a method, document non-salvageable items for your claim rather than charging to clean them, and tell you honestly when replacement is the better path.

Cost and insurance considerations

The cost of fire damage restoration depends on the size of the fire, the type of smoke residue, how far it traveled through the building and how much of the structure and contents can be cleaned rather than replaced.

Fire is a named peril on standard policies. Expect the claim to involve a structural estimate, a contents inventory and, if you cannot stay in the home, additional living expense coverage.

Fire Damage in Pensacola: FAQs

Will insurance pay for a hotel while my house is repaired?

Most homeowners policies include additional living expense coverage for temporary housing and extra costs while the home is uninhabitable, up to the policy limit. Keep every receipt.

Is it safe to clean up soot myself?

Light soot on hard surfaces can be dry-sponged, but wet cleaning usually smears it deeper, and soot is a respiratory irritant that may carry toxic combustion byproducts. Significant soot or any odor problem is best handled professionally.

Does my HVAC system need cleaning after a fire?

Almost always. Ducts, the air handler and filters collect soot and redistribute it every time the system runs. Cleaning the system and replacing filters is standard in smoke restoration.

My neighbor had a fire and my place smells like smoke. Can you help?

Yes. Smoke that migrated through shared walls, windows or vents still leaves acidic residue and odor. Cleaning and deodorizing are covered by most policies as smoke damage, and local crews handle these losses regularly in Pensacola.

Can I choose my own restoration contractor?

In nearly all cases, yes. Be cautious of anyone who shows up uninvited at a fire scene. Take the time to call a company you have vetted and get the scope in writing.
