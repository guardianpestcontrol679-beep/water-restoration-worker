What causes water, fire and mold damage in Bonita Springs

Local conditions in Bonita Springs follow the broader pattern across Florida. Florida's year-round humidity, intense afternoon storms and hurricane season create the most mold-friendly conditions in the country, and air-conditioning condensate leaks are one of the most common sources of interior water damage. Storm surge, roof damage and wind-driven rain during tropical systems account for many of the largest losses. Problems tend to peak during hurricane season (june through november), but plumbing failures and appliance leaks happen in every month of the year.

Typical emergencies in the area:

  • Tidal and coastal flooding in low-lying neighborhoods, plus salt-air corrosion of plumbing
  • Clogged air-conditioning condensate lines and overflowing drain pans that soak ceilings and closets
  • Pinhole leaks in copper lines inside walls
  • Washing machine hose bursts and dishwasher or refrigerator supply line leaks
  • Wind-driven rain, roof damage and storm surge from hurricanes and tropical storms
  • Persistent humidity that slows natural drying and lets damp materials turn into mold problems
  • Electrical shorts in humid attics and crawlspaces
  • Electrical fires when power is restored to water-damaged wiring after a storm
  • Salt air and coastal humidity that keep building materials damp
  • Air-conditioning condensate leaks and sweating ductwork in closets and attics

What to do right now

The first hour determines how much can be saved. While a crew is on the way:

  • Open your insurance claim early; mitigation is a policy condition and delays can reduce what is paid
  • Move rugs, furniture legs and valuables out of the water and off wet floors
  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything
  • After a fire, wait for the fire department to release the property and do not wipe soot with water, which smears it into surfaces
  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a plumbing failure, and turn off power to the affected area if water is near outlets or appliances
  • If you see or smell mold, avoid disturbing it and keep the area closed off until it is assessed
