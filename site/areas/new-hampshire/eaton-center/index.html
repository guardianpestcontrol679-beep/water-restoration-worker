What causes water, fire and mold damage in Eaton Center

Eaton Center shares the weather and building patterns of the rest of the state. New Hampshire's cold winters, heavy snow and spring mud season make frozen pipes, ice dams and wet basements the dominant water problems, often in older homes and seasonal properties that sit unheated. Nor'easters bring wind-driven rain and power outages that disable sump pumps. Problems tend to peak during winter and the spring thaw, but plumbing failures and appliance leaks happen in every month of the year.

Typical emergencies in the area:

  • Split supply lines that only start leaking once a hard freeze thaws
  • Frozen and burst pipes during winter cold snaps, especially lines in exterior walls, attics, garages and crawlspaces
  • Basement seepage, sump pump failures and sewer backups through floor drains
  • Fire-suppression water after a kitchen or electrical fire
  • Roof leaks around flashing, vents and chimneys
  • Ice dams that back melting snow under shingles and into ceilings and exterior walls
  • Furnace malfunctions at the start of heating season
  • Electrical fires from aging wiring, overloaded circuits and outdated panels
  • Meltwater seepage into foundations each spring
  • Bathroom fans venting into cold attics

Before the crew arrives

A few actions in the first minutes limit the damage and protect your claim:

  • If you see or smell mold, avoid disturbing it and keep the area closed off until it is assessed
  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything
  • Open your insurance claim early; mitigation is a policy condition and delays can reduce what is paid
  • Do not run the HVAC system if ducts or the air handler got wet, and do not use household fans on sewage or flood water
  • After a fire, wait for the fire department to release the property and do not wipe soot with water, which smears it into surfaces
  • Stay out of standing water that could be electrically charged or contaminated with sewage

Does homeowners insurance in New Hampshire cover water damage?

Standard policies generally cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or appliance failure. Flooding from rising water, long-term leaks and neglected maintenance are usually excluded, and sewer backup often requires an endorsement. Check your specific policy or ask your agent.
