Restoration services available throughout New Hampshire

  • Water damage restoration: extraction, structural drying, mold prevention and rebuild after burst pipes, appliance failures, roof leaks and flooding.
  • Fire damage restoration: emergency board-up, suppression water removal, soot and smoke cleaning, odor elimination and reconstruction.
  • Mold remediation: inspection, containment, HEPA filtration, safe removal and moisture correction under the IICRC S520 standard.
  • Sewage, storm and biohazard cleanup: contaminated-water protocols, disinfection and lawful disposal.

What to expect from a New Hampshire restoration crew

Every crew we connect you with in New Hampshire is a locally operated company. Expect a call back within minutes, an arrival window measured in hours for active emergencies, a moisture or damage assessment on arrival, a written scope and photo documentation that supports your claim. You are free to ask about licensing and certifications, and you should.

Insurance notes for New Hampshire property owners

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and fire, exclude flooding from rising water (which requires a separate flood policy) and gradual leaks, and often treat sewer backup and mold as optional or limited coverages. Frozen pipe claims may be denied if a home was left unheated, so check your policy's freeze provisions before winter travel.

New Hampshire restoration FAQs

Are the restoration companies in New Hampshire licensed?

They are independent businesses responsible for their own licensing and insurance. Ask for proof of liability insurance, any license New Hampshire requires for the work (some states license mold remediators specifically) and IICRC certification.

How quickly do crews respond in New Hampshire?

Active water and fire emergencies are typically scheduled for same-day response, often within hours. Response in rural parts of New Hampshire may take longer because crews cover a wider radius, so call as early as possible.

When is damage most common in New Hampshire?

Winter and the spring thaw is the busiest period for restoration crews in New Hampshire. Nor'easters bring wind-driven rain and power outages that disable sump pumps.
