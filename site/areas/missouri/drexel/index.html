Common property damage problems in Drexel

Local conditions in Drexel follow the broader pattern across Missouri. Missouri sees heavy rain, flash floods and major river flooding along the Missouri and Mississippi, along with tornadoes, hail and winter ice storms. Basements are standard in much of the state, so groundwater seepage and sump failures are everyday calls. Problems tend to peak during spring storm and flood season, but plumbing failures and appliance leaks happen in every month of the year.

The calls crews receive most often from this area include:

  • Hail-punctured roofs and skylights that leak during the next rain
  • Pinhole leaks in copper lines inside walls
  • Tornado and straight-line wind damage that opens roofs and walls to rain
  • Flash flooding that pushes water through doors, window wells and foundation walls in minutes
  • Toilet and tub overflows
  • River flooding when nearby waterways crest after prolonged rain
  • Lightning-caused attic and roof fires
  • Electrical shorts in humid attics and crawlspaces
  • Indoor humidity that stays above 60 percent for much of the year
  • Bathroom fans venting into cold attics

What to do right now

A few actions in the first minutes limit the damage and protect your claim:

  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a plumbing failure, and turn off power to the affected area if water is near outlets or appliances
  • Open your insurance claim early; mitigation is a policy condition and delays can reduce what is paid
  • If you see or smell mold, avoid disturbing it and keep the area closed off until it is assessed
  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything
  • Do not run the HVAC system if ducts or the air handler got wet, and do not use household fans on sewage or flood water
  • Stay out of standing water that could be electrically charged or contaminated with sewage
