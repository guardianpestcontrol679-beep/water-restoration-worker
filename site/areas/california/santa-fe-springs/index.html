What causes water, fire and mold damage in Santa Fe Springs

Santa Fe Springs shares the weather and building patterns of the rest of the state. California's property damage risks are unusually varied: wildfire and smoke damage in the wildland-urban interface, atmospheric-river storms that overwhelm roofs and drainage in winter, post-fire debris flows, and the everyday slab leaks and supply-line failures found in slab-on-grade housing. Older housing stock in coastal cities adds aging plumbing and wiring to the list. Problems tend to peak during wildfire season in late summer and fall, storm season in winter, but plumbing failures and appliance leaks happen in every month of the year.

The calls crews receive most often from this area include:

  • Roof leaks around flashing, vents and chimneys
  • Plumbing connections and water heaters shaken loose by seismic movement
  • Flash flooding that pushes water through doors, window wells and foundation walls in minutes
  • Slab leaks from supply lines running beneath concrete foundations
  • Water heater tank failures that release 40 to 80 gallons at once
  • Tidal and coastal flooding in low-lying neighborhoods, plus salt-air corrosion of plumbing
  • Lightning-caused attic and roof fires
  • Electrical fires from aging wiring, overloaded circuits and outdated panels
  • Slow plumbing leaks inside the walls of older homes
  • Slab leaks that keep flooring and bottom plates damp for months

First steps for Santa Fe Springs property owners

A few actions in the first minutes limit the damage and protect your claim:

  • Move rugs, furniture legs and valuables out of the water and off wet floors
  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a plumbing failure, and turn off power to the affected area if water is near outlets or appliances
  • Open your insurance claim early; mitigation is a policy condition and delays can reduce what is paid
  • If you see or smell mold, avoid disturbing it and keep the area closed off until it is assessed
  • After a fire, wait for the fire department to release the property and do not wipe soot with water, which smears it into surfaces
  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything

What should I do about a sewage backup in Santa Fe Springs?
