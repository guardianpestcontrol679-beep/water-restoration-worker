Restoration services available throughout North Carolina

  • Water damage restoration: extraction, structural drying, mold prevention and rebuild after burst pipes, appliance failures, roof leaks and flooding.
  • Fire damage restoration: emergency board-up, suppression water removal, soot and smoke cleaning, odor elimination and reconstruction.
  • Mold remediation: inspection, containment, HEPA filtration, safe removal and moisture correction under the IICRC S520 standard.
  • Sewage, storm and biohazard cleanup: contaminated-water protocols, disinfection and lawful disposal.

What to expect from a North Carolina restoration crew

Restoration companies in North Carolina are independent local businesses. When you call (888) 755-3821, we route you to one that covers your ZIP code and has crews available. They assess the damage, stop further loss, give you a written scope and document everything for your insurance claim. Confirm licensing, insurance and IICRC certification before work begins, as you would with any contractor.

Insurance notes for North Carolina property owners

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and fire, exclude flooding from rising water (which requires a separate flood policy) and gradual leaks, and often treat sewer backup and mold as optional or limited coverages. In hurricane-exposed areas, policies may carry separate wind or hurricane deductibles, so review your declarations page.

North Carolina restoration FAQs

Does Water Restoration handle commercial properties in North Carolina?

Yes. Offices, retail, restaurants, warehouses, multifamily and other commercial buildings across North Carolina are served by companies with commercial-scale crews and equipment.

When is damage most common in North Carolina?

Hurricane season, with winter ice storms is the busiest period for restoration crews in North Carolina. North Carolina's coast and inland river basins flood during hurricanes and tropical storms, the mountains see flash floods and landslides, and the Piedmont's crawlspace homes trap humidity that leads to mold. Summer thunderstorms and ice storms add roof and tree damage.

Are the restoration companies in North Carolina licensed?

They are independent businesses responsible for their own licensing and insurance. Ask for proof of liability insurance, any license North Carolina requires for the work (some states license mold remediators specifically) and IICRC certification.

How quickly do crews respond in North Carolina?

Active water and fire emergencies are typically scheduled for same-day response, often within hours. Response in rural parts of North Carolina may take longer because crews cover a wider radius, so call as early as possible.
