Common property damage problems in Post

Post shares the weather and building patterns of the rest of the state. Western Oregon's long rainy season saturates crawlspaces and finds every roof and window flashing flaw, while the east side of the Cascades deals with hard freezes and wildfire. Smoke damage from large summer wildfires affects communities statewide. Problems tend to peak during the october-to-april rainy season and late-summer fire season, but plumbing failures and appliance leaks happen in every month of the year.

In Post, the most frequent emergencies look like this:

  • Spring snowmelt that saturates the soil and seeps into basements and foundations
  • Water heater tank failures that release 40 to 80 gallons at once
  • Split supply lines that only start leaking once a hard freeze thaws
  • Standing water and wet insulation in crawlspaces
  • Plumbing connections and water heaters shaken loose by seismic movement
  • Leaking shower pans and failed tile grout
  • Wildfire smoke, ash and soot infiltration, and structure fires at the wildland edge
  • Space heater, fireplace and chimney fires during cold weather
  • Meltwater seepage into foundations each spring
  • Bathroom fans venting into cold attics

What to do right now

A few actions in the first minutes limit the damage and protect your claim:

  • Photograph and video the damage before moving anything
  • Do not run the HVAC system if ducts or the air handler got wet, and do not use household fans on sewage or flood water
  • After a fire, wait for the fire department to release the property and do not wipe soot with water, which smears it into surfaces
  • Stay out of standing water that could be electrically charged or contaminated with sewage
  • Shut off the main water valve if the source is a plumbing failure, and turn off power to the affected area if water is near outlets or appliances
  • Open your insurance claim early; mitigation is a policy condition and delays can reduce what is paid

Building types, timing and seasonal risk

Regardless of the age of a building, water finds the same weak points: supply connections, roof penetrations, window flashing and anywhere two materials meet.

Winter brings frozen and burst pipes; spring and summer bring the heavy rain, saturated ground and sump failures that flood lower levels. Home fires peak in winter, when space heaters, fireplaces and furnaces are running and holiday cooking and decorating add risk. Winter condensation on windows and in attics, followed by spring moisture in basements and crawlspaces, gives mold two seasons to get started.
