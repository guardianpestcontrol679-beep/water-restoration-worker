Restoration services available throughout Tennessee

  • Water damage restoration: extraction, structural drying, mold prevention and rebuild after burst pipes, appliance failures, roof leaks and flooding.
  • Fire damage restoration: emergency board-up, suppression water removal, soot and smoke cleaning, odor elimination and reconstruction.
  • Mold remediation: inspection, containment, HEPA filtration, safe removal and moisture correction under the IICRC S520 standard.
  • Sewage, storm and biohazard cleanup: contaminated-water protocols, disinfection and lawful disposal.

What to expect from a Tennessee restoration crew

Every crew we connect you with in Tennessee is a locally operated company. Expect a call back within minutes, an arrival window measured in hours for active emergencies, a moisture or damage assessment on arrival, a written scope and photo documentation that supports your claim. You are free to ask about licensing and certifications, and you should.

Insurance notes for Tennessee property owners

Standard homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and fire, exclude flooding from rising water (which requires a separate flood policy) and gradual leaks, and often treat sewer backup and mold as optional or limited coverages. Frozen pipe claims may be denied if a home was left unheated, so check your policy's freeze provisions before winter travel.

Tennessee restoration FAQs

How quickly do crews respond in Tennessee?

Active water and fire emergencies are typically scheduled for same-day response, often within hours. Response in rural parts of Tennessee may take longer because crews cover a wider radius, so call as early as possible.

Does Water Restoration handle commercial properties in Tennessee?

Yes. Offices, retail, restaurants, warehouses, multifamily and other commercial buildings across Tennessee are served by companies with commercial-scale crews and equipment.

Are the restoration companies in Tennessee licensed?

They are independent businesses responsible for their own licensing and insurance. Ask for proof of liability insurance, any license Tennessee requires for the work (some states license mold remediators specifically) and IICRC certification.

When is damage most common in Tennessee?

Spring storm season and winter ice is the busiest period for restoration crews in Tennessee. Tennessee sees some of the most intense rainfall and flash flooding in the Southeast, along with tornado outbreaks, ice storms and humid summers that keep crawlspaces damp. Much of the state's housing sits over crawlspaces or partial basements where moisture problems start.
